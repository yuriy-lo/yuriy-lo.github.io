TL;DR: don't read r/cybersecurity at night if you want to sleep well.

A few headlines from today:

GitHub investigates internal repositories breach claimed by TeamPCP. Hackers spent nearly 3 months inside the New York City health system before anyone noticed. BitLocker-protected drives can now be opened with just some files on a USB stick. AI-powered bug hunters have made Linux security mailing list ‘almost entirely unmanageable’. NIST is surrendering to the amount of CVEs coming in.

I am a well-mannered person, and I will not use four-letter words. But how do you continue business when one vulnerable VS Code extension, stolen laptop, or outdated third-party component can disrupt it sooner or later?

Plan business growth as if your source code had been open-sourced this morning. Switch your stack to Linux; sandboxing of development environments is easier there. Sell your products like the deal closes while SRE is already shutting down customer-facing services. Use IaC, you will thank yourself later. Invest in employee training and don't blindly trust vendors.

And never stop. There's no better recipe than keeping learning and improving.
